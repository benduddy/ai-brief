Bearish

Cash does not arrive until late 2027, so for two years the stock still trades mostly with Bitcoin. The Texas 885 MW site is still an LOI with exclusivity, not a signed lease. There is no GPU-rental deal, so the upside is capped at landlord economics.

Valuation

Forward P/E is negative (still loss-making on forward estimates) even with ~+30% expected earnings growth, so the PEG screen doesn't apply. The honest valuation now is the lease: ~$330M a year of contracted income from 2028 versus today's equity plus the new debt.

Forward view

By 2028 Sandersville alone should add ~$330M a year of steady rent; a signed Texas lease on similar terms would be several times that. I don't think the market has fully priced this: the stock is still ~42% below its 52-week high of $23.20, and it moved only one ATR on the news. The discount reflects the gap to 2027 and the debt, not doubt about the tenant.
