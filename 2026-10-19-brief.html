What the smart money is saying (from the wiki)

Jordi Visser (18 Oct, same thesis on Pompliano 17 Oct): the AI bubble did not pop; hedge-fund leverage did. He compares it to a crowded, levered trade unwinding, and expects pain through hyperscaler earnings and maybe the midterms, then a recovery by year-end. He also walked back his 3 Oct line that AI has no rate sensitivity. Jensen Huang (Bg2, 15 Oct): AI supply is constrained through 2027, so demand is not the problem; he sells the chips, so weigh that. InvestAnswers (14–15 Oct): blames AI credit fear and CoreWeave. The first two are one person saying one thing twice, so count them as one independent view. My read from the wiki, medium confidence that leverage explains part of the move and low that it explains all of it: shakeout if NVDA rises after the late-October hyperscaler capex prints; reset if it stays flat on strong numbers. Falsifier for the shakeout reading: high-yield spreads through 3.46% or a neocloud refinancing priced wide.

How to read this: neutral, balanced read — bull and bear side by side. The Setup lines are opinions on risk/reward and timing to weigh for yourself, not directives; the call is yours.

Score key: Heat 0 = far below its highs and its 50-day (damaged; historically these fell further), 100 = a strong run near its highs (historically no extra pullback risk). Momentum = strength of the recent up-move. Status: Leading = strong and healthy; Overheated = strong and stretched (not a pullback signal); Basing = weak, not yet a bottom; Neutral = no clear signal. ATR = the name's normal daily swing. Forward PEG = forward P/E ÷ expected EPS growth; under ~1 cheap for its growth, over ~2 expensive. Entry grades A/B/C: the name clears both momentum gates; the letter is position against short-term averages, not a rank. D: no momentum edge yet.
