Market backdrop

A Fed hike, a chip rally, and a leadership row change

The day

The market's marquee event came and went: the Fed raised its policy rate by 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on a 12–0 vote, and the projections show most officials expect at least one more hike this year. The AI complex rallied through it — AMD, Marvell, Dell and Intel were all up ~4–5% and optics led. The model logged 57 row changes and 4 status flips. The S&P 500 sits ~3.2% below its high; the 58-name AI universe sits ~32% below its own 60-day highs on average, a gap that is just outside the model's reporting band today.

Macro regime

Neutral, unchanged — the expected setting most days, and neither an all-clear nor a warning. The inputs are mixed rather than stressed: high-yield spreads ~2.76% (real FRED series), up only ~0.05 over 50 days; VIX ~16; the 10-year real rate ~2.6%; the dollar ~3% above its 200-day. The 2s10s curve is ~0.27% and flattening, so the liquidity gate (G1) stays shut — which does not mean the AI thesis is broken. Monthly falsifier check (GPU rental, DRAM spot) is next due 1 Oct.

Leadership scorecard (Hunter vs the fundamentals camp)

Financials have outrun semis by ~6 points and materials by ~4 over the last 50 sessions, with semis themselves down ~6%; high-yield spreads have widened slightly over the same window (~+0.05). On paper that is both legs of David Hunter's late-cycle signature — value leading and credit worsening — so the tape currently rhymes with his script. The honest weight is low: the credit move is tiny and spreads are still tight in absolute terms, and one strong semis session (like yesterday) narrows the spread fast. Software (IGV ~105) is not near the ~110 level Hunter named as a possible top. A scorecard on someone else's call, not a signal of our own.

My read: the optics leg (LITE/COHR) is where the wiki's supply-constraint thesis and the tape now agree most clearly.
