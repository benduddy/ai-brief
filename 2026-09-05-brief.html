How to read this

The read

Neutral and balanced — bull and bear side by side. The Setup lines are opinions on risk/reward and timing to weigh for yourself, not directives; the call is yours. One standing disclaimer here does for the whole brief.

Scores

Heat 0 = washed-out/cheap-looking, 100 = red-hot/overextended. Momentum = strength of the recent up-move. Status: Leading = strong & healthy, Overheated = strong but stretched, Basing = weak/bottoming, Neutral = no clear signal.

Entry grades

A = leading, trend-intact and sitting at a buyable entry (at or near its 21-day average). B = the same but slightly extended. C = a strong trend at a hot or below-the-line entry — wait, don't chase. D = no momentum edge yet. Grades run on the real trend fields (200-day, 50/200-day cross, 12-month return, EMA-based pullback zone) — a timing lens, not a verdict on the business. Not financial advice.

Strong & still cheap-ish

Market backdrop & what the smart money is saying

Backdrop

Tape

Thursday (4 Sep) was a broad melt-up in the AI-compute core: memory (SNDK/WDC/STX/MU), custom silicon (MRVL), and neoclouds (NBIS/CRWV/IREN) all ripped, with ten status upgrades to Leading. That is a change of character from the past week, when the risk-on move was concentrated in crypto and miners while the compute leaders sat quiet. Tesla was the notable exception, down ~6%. Today's fresh event: the August jobs report (out this morning) came in firm, which pushes real yields and the dollar up a touch and trims Fed rate-cut odds into the 16–17 Sep FOMC — a headwind the Thursday tape below does not yet reflect.

Macro regime

The regime still reads debasement for a second day, but the read has softened. Gold has slipped to ~2% below its 200-day average (it was roughly flat yesterday), so the debasement signature now rests almost entirely on the divergence flag — gold vs a rising real 10-year yield (~2.42%, +0.06 over 50 days) — rather than on any gold uptrend. The dollar is flat vs its 200-day, and high-yield credit is calm (spread 2.65%, tightening slightly, on the real FRED series). Per the rotation framework this is still the thesis-friendly path, but a firm jobs print today argues the other way. Treat it as a pay-attention flag that is fading — not a call, and certainly not an exit signal.

Portfolio vs index

Worth noting the gap: the S&P 500 sits near its record highs while the 58-name AI universe is, on average, ~31% below its own 60-day highs. (The aggregate now sweeps lower-beta Application-Layer names too, so it is not directly comparable to the −44%/−50% figures from the August backtest — quote the live number for the live gap.)

Smart money — from the wiki

The followed commentators still favour infrastructure over hyperscalers, and memory is the highest-conviction leg. Jordi Visser: data-centre construction delays "don't really matter, because we don't have enough memory anyway" — memory, not construction, is the binding constraint. Dylan Patel (SemiAnalysis) adds an independent "bullwhip" mechanism: as the top of the compute stack raises prices, DRAM/HBM reprices up behind it. Both point the same way, and Thursday's memory breakout is the tape starting to agree. On Marvell, the wiki call is that its mega-cap path is a custom-XPU platform story (the new Google deal is the first hard proof), not a laser pure-play — Broadcom, not Lumentum, is the real rival.

Accumulation watch

Beyond the buyable leaders, the "weak tape, strong future" screen flags names basing now but cheap for strong forward growth — AVGO, AMD, COHR, GEV, ALAB, GLW. Low momentum is the feature, not the bug (you're early); most are below their 50-day without a firm base, so scale in rather than lump in.
