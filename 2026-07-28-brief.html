  • Monday was a washout, and this is the first real look at it. The signal snapshot (07:22 UTC) captures Monday's close — the first fresh equity tape since Friday. Nearly the whole 48-name book fell, semiconductors and optical/networking hardest. Ten status flips, almost all downgrades.
  • Two fresh China catalysts stacked on top of the capex fear. The pullback the 'wait-for-it' leaders needed has, mostly, arrived — several are now sitting on or just under their 50-day average.
  • Weak tape, strong future — a value-accumulation cohort just got cheaper. The sell-off left a group of names with strong forward-growth estimates trading at low forward PEGs — Micron (PEG ~0.05), Marvell (~0.56), the optical trio (LITE/COHR/AAOI). Low momentum here is a feature, not a disqualifier — but most are still mid-fall, so this is accumulate-into-weakness in tranches, not a bottom call.
  • Straight into the busiest week of the quarter. The FOMC decides Wednesday 2pm ET — a hold is still base case but hike odds have jumped to ~46% (from ~34% Sunday, partly on a fresh Hormuz oil-supply scare). Microsoft + Meta report Wednesday after the close, Amazon + Apple Thursday — the four prints that decide whether Friday's Alphabet/Tesla capex shock was company-specific or industry-wide.

What happened

Monday deepened the AI-spending doubt that started with Friday's Alphabet and Tesla selloff. The tech sector was the worst performer; chipmakers led the way down, and the pain was global — SK Hynix fell as much as 13%, Samsung ~10%, dragging Korea's Kospi down 9%. Even Nvidia lost ~6%. Jim Cramer flagged the sellers as 'monstrous, motivated and often margined' — i.e. forced, leveraged selling, the same mechanic that drove the Korea memory unwind a couple of weeks ago.

The China angle

Two separate China stories poured fuel on it. First, a report that a Shanghai state-backed firm has begun mass-producing homegrown DUV lithography machines — a step Western toolmakers assumed they still controlled — which is a direct shot at the semi-equipment moat (ASML) and the broader chip complex. Second, memory maker CXMT (ChangXin) IPO'd and rocketed ~466% on day one; analysts see its global DRAM share rising toward ~10% in 2026, a real threat in commodity DRAM even if the high-margin AI memory (HBM) prize still favours Micron.

The rotation

The flip side: money didn't leave the market, it rotated. Materials (+1.9%) and real estate (+2.2%) were the day's best sectors while tech sank — a textbook risk-off rotation out of the crowded AI names into cheaper, more defensive cyclicals. That single fact explains most of the book below: it's why Steel Dynamics is the only green leader and why the semis and optical names are the ones bleeding.

The macro week

The Fed decides Wednesday at 2pm ET. A 'hawkish hold' at 3.50–3.75% is still the base case, but the odds of a 0.25% hike have climbed to ~46% (from ~34% Sunday) — pushed up partly by a fresh Strait-of-Hormuz oil-supply scare that raises the inflation risk. It's a non-forecast meeting (no new dot plot). Then the capex gauntlet: Microsoft + Meta Wednesday after the close, Amazon + Apple Thursday. Those four prints test whether Friday's capex shock was Google-specific or an industry-wide 'the spend is too high' verdict.

Bullish

The single best story in the book today: STLD rose ~4% and flipped from Basing to Leading (Leading = strong & healthy) while the AI names sank. It's the direct beneficiary of the rotation into materials, and it has the fundamentals to back it — Q2 (reported 20 July) delivered record steel shipments of 3.7M tons, $6.1B sales and adjusted EPS of $3.80, nearly double the $2.01 a year ago and ahead of estimates.

Bearish

It's leading precisely because money is fleeing the AI trade — so a chunk of today's strength is rotation flow that can reverse just as fast if semis stabilise and the money rotates back. It's also now near its 52-week high (~$282) with the least room to fall of any name here, so you're buying strength, not a discount.

Valuation

Forward P/E ~13.3 on ~14% next-year earnings growth — a forward PEG of ~0.98 (forward PEG = price/earnings divided by expected growth; under ~1 means you're paying little for the growth, over ~2 means the opposite). So it's fairly priced for its growth, and cheap in absolute terms versus the 20–40x AI names — part of why it's catching the rotation.

Forward view

The forward kicker isn't steel, it's aluminium: management expects its new aluminium mills (two of three cold mills running, annealing lines qualifying) to ramp volumes and profitability sharply in H2 2026 as startup costs fade. That's a second earnings engine the current multiple isn't fully paying for yet — a real forward story hiding behind a 'boring steel' label.
