Black Friday bounce: chips and optics rip in a half-day, Bitcoin keeps sliding

28 November 2026 · 68 names · benchmark QQQ · data US stocks: Fri 27 Nov close (1 pm ET early close) · crypto: Sat 28 Nov ~06:00 UTC · published 06:06 UTC

A short, strong session. In Friday's half-day the Nasdaq rose 2.3% and the chip index nearly 5%, the third big swing in a volatile week. Optics and memory led: Coherent +14%, Applied Opto +10%, Micron +7.6%. The press found no company news behind any of it; one strategist called it a technical bounce. Two names flipped to Leading: Qualcomm and Amazon. Nvidia barely moved (+0.8%). Crypto went the other way, with Bitcoin down 2.8% to ~$59.7k after a large options expiry and record ETF outflows for November. Credit still reads stressed, and a December Fed hike is ~98% priced.

  • Macro regime: deflationary-stress, day 10 — unchanged. High-yield spreads 3.77% (FRED), +0.52 points in 50 days and up from 3.72%. VIX 23.8, down 11% on the day. The dollar is 3.9% above its 200-day; the real 10-year rate is 2.73%. A pay-attention flag, not an exit signal.
  • The bounce was broad and had no named cause. S&P +1.6% to 7,297, Nasdaq +2.3% to 24,130 (Yahoo Finance, 27 Nov). The S&P is still 6.7% under its high. 56 of our 59 stocks with a clean row rose. XTB's Kathleen Brooks: the causes of the panic "remain unanswered" (via Motley Fool).
  • Two status flips: Qualcomm Basing → Leading (entry grade B, PEG ~0.55: the only "strong and still cheap-ish" name today) and Amazon Neutral → Leading (grade C, back over its 200-day).
  • Arista is the one confirmed value candidate. Cheap for its growth (PEG ~0.76) and now back above its 200-day (~$169). Every other name on the value list is still under its 200-day.
  • Bitcoin is the outlier: −2.8%, closed on its low, heat 0. ~$7B of Deribit options expired Friday; November spot-ETF outflows were a record ~$4.9–5.4B (crypto.news, secondary). Still cheap on holder cost (MVRV 1.13), but under its 200-day (~$71.8k).
  • Data warning — Sandisk: our file shows SNDK at $94.39 and roughly flat. The press reports +16% to $85.36 after the 8-for-1 split (19 Nov). Our SNDK row looks wrong; it is left out today.
  • Next week is heavy: Marvell reports Tue 1 Dec (options imply a 13–16% move). Huang's House interview the same day; Senate hearing reported for 3 Dec. Jobs report Fri 4 Dec. Fed meets 8–9 Dec.

Macro regime

Tenth day of deflationary-stress, not a new call. High-yield spreads 3.77% on FRED, up from 3.72% and +0.52 points over 50 days: lenders still want more pay for risk, even on an up day for stocks. The dollar is 3.9% above its 200-day (DXY +1.9% in November, Yahoo). VIX fell to 23.8. In the rotation framework this is the "deflationary bust" column, where a rotation into BTC fails; BTC fell on Friday, which fits. The gate fires often with close to no edge, so read it as "conditions rhyme with past stress", not as a call.

Rates and the Fed

The 10-year rose ~6–8bp Friday to ~4.93% and was flat on the week; one wrap has it lower (TradingNews, conflicting). The 2s10s curve is 0.25 on our file (22bp per Yahoo, the flattest since February): falsifier G1 stays shut. A 25bp December hike is ~98% priced, and two hikes by March (CME FedWatch via secondary sources; Stocktwits/Yahoo). October core PCE was 3.3%.

Credit and financing

No new item dated 26–28 Nov. The latest levels (Bloomberg via a secondary source, 25 Nov data): CoreWeave 5-year credit-default swaps 1,314bp, Oracle 356bp, SoftBank 501bp. Yesterday's brief quoted 1,452bp and 371bp from ZeroHedge for the same days, so the sources disagree on the level, not the direction. Banks plan to sell a $4.5B CoreWeave loan backed by a Meta contract at ~7–7.5% (Quartz/Bloomberg, ~25 Nov).

Nvidia and export rules

Nvidia rose only 0.8% on a 5% chip day. Per the FT (via TechCrunch, 26 Nov), Nvidia told server makers incl. Dell and Super Micro to halt shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand under the licence rule in force since 19 Nov; it is not clear whether the halt is temporary. An unverified headline (Invezz, 27 Nov) says NVDA gave back its opening gain on reports of Blackwell chips at a Chinese data centre. Dell still rose 6.7%. Huang's closed-door House interview is Tue 1 Dec; the Senate Banking hearing (3 Dec) is reported but not final (Forbes).
