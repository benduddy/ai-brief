The tape

Almost every name closed in the bottom tenth of its day’s range — no late buyers.

Why, per the press

CNBC (20 Nov) lists five forces: (1) the Anthropic IPO pulled cash out of other AI names; (2) Nvidia — CEO exit, SEC accounting probe, tighter export rules; (3) the S&P lost its 200-day average, which starts automatic selling by trend-following funds; (4) the Fed — Governor Waller raised the idea of a 0.5-point hike in December, and markets price hikes in December and January; (5) slower earnings growth. I report this list; I did not test which force mattered most.

Macro regime

Fifth day of deflationary-stress. High-yield spreads widened to 3.52% (FRED; rising spreads mean lenders want more pay for risk), +0.37 points over 50 days. VIX 35.9, up from 28.4. Dollar 3.8% above its 200-day; real 10-year rate 2.88%. Gold fell ~3% to ~$3,517 (Motley Fool), so there is no debasement signal. In the rotation framework this is the “deflationary bust” column, where a rotation into BTC fails — and BTC fell 10%. The gate fires often with close to no edge; read it as “conditions rhyme with past stress”. Lagging context: NFCI −0.48 as of 13 Nov (looser than average), so financial conditions had not yet tightened a week ago.

Earnings and events

Anthropic, week one: priced at $83, traded above $115 on Thursday, closed the week at $88.91 — still above the IPO price (CNBC, 20 Nov). CNBC also says Anthropic’s Q3 revenue growth was 47%, down from 144% in Q2. I could not check that figure against the prospectus.

Connecting it: two days ago the market paid a record price for an AI lab. On Friday it sold the chip makers, the neoclouds and the miners hardest. The selling hit the names with the most borrowed money and the most crowding first: miners and neoclouds fell 16–19% over two sessions, Nvidia 14%, insurers rose. That pattern fits forced selling more than a change in AI demand. The demand gauges in our file still say intact: the H100 rental index rose 5.2% in 4 weeks (18 Nov capture), and no hyperscaler has cut capex in words (F3).

Falsifier watch

F4 — Nvidia leadership/financing: intact, on alert. It needs two of four legs. (1) Leak plus policy response: the policy part is there — Commerce tightened export licences for Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil on 16 Nov, and Fortune ties it to the Nvidia scandal — but no leak is confirmed. (2) Financing pullback: none reported. (3) Guidance cut: no — the Q4 guide is $130B. (4) Messy CEO search: the board ruled out Huang’s return (CNBC); no report of a messy search. The new item is outside the list: the SEC probe of Nvidia’s accounting, after the delayed 10-Q and the board’s own investigation. If the probe touches revenue recognition on the customer-financing deals, leg 2 and the accounting story become one story. F1–F3 intact; G1 gate shut (2s10s 0.29), no change.

What the smart money is saying (from the wiki)

No new followed-source material since the last brief. Visser (14 Nov) said the buildout does not depend on Huang and expected Nvidia’s numbers to hold. The numbers held; the price did not, and no followed source has covered an accounting risk. The wiki’s own 12-Nov bear case named vendor financing as the key channel — an accounting probe is where that channel would show up first.

My read

This is a forced-selling flush inside a real stress regime, not yet a demand break. Evidence for: borrowed-money names fell most, defensives rose, and the demand gauges (GPU rental, capex language) are intact. Evidence against: credit spreads, VIX and the Fed all point the wrong way, and an accounting question on the sector’s largest company can change what the earnings are worth. I see no sign of a bottom yet: no name closed off its low, and BTC lost the support the AI trade usually gets from crypto. Conviction: medium. What would prove me wrong: a hyperscaler cuts capex in words (F3), the GPU rental index turns down (F1), or the SEC probe touches revenue on the financing deals.
