A risk-on close. The S&P 500 rose ~0.65% to a record ~7,799 and the Nasdaq ~0.81%, after July producer prices came in flat — soft enough that markets now put ~63% odds on the Fed holding rates steady in September (and the small chance of a hike is fading). Bond yields fell, small caps hit a record, and the leadership inside AI rotated hard: memory/storage led while optical and crypto lagged. This was a broad, calm move, not a single-name spike.

What the smart money is saying (from the wiki)

Today's tape is the optics-vs-memory debate resolving toward memory — the same argument that knocked Coherent on 10-Aug is now flowing the other way. The wiki's memory page has argued the market keeps mis-reading soft memory guides as "the peak" when they are really long-term supply agreements (floors and caps that trade away peak margin to kill the old boom-bust cycle). SanDisk's Investor Day made that case out loud. Gavin Baker (4-Aug) frames memory as "the single most important lever" for tokens-per-chip, with near-zero demand elasticity; UBS (via Milk Road, 11-Aug) models DRAM margins rising into 2028 and HBM into 2029; and Nvidia is reportedly cutting memory content in its next platform ~50% because it cannot source enough. Jordi Visser's standing read — prefer the supply layers (memory, optical, power) over the hyperscalers, and treat rotations inside the basket as "your capex is my opportunity" — fits the split cleanly. Weigh it: these are structurally-bullish sources; the honest bear case is that memory is still ultimately cyclical and this is the market front-running an eventual 2027-28 supply catch-up.
