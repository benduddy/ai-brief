What happened

The sell-off that began with Friday's Alphabet and Tesla capex shock and broke wide open Monday kept going through the week. Semiconductors fell for four sessions running, the Nasdaq-100 shed roughly another ~1.8%, and the memory and optical/networking names took the worst of it. The frozen snapshots have now caught up, so this is the first clean look at the mid-week damage — and the book is broadly lower than the Monday close the last brief was built on. Nothing in the 48 names is overheated; heat has washed out to near zero almost everywhere.

The capex prints

This is what the week was really about. Microsoft (reported Wednesday after the close) guided FY2027 capital spending to $255–260B, about a 35% step up — a bet that AI demand keeps compounding. Meta beat on revenue (~$60.8B, +28%) but its Q2 AI capex doubled to ~$31B and free cash flow fell ~91%, and its shares dropped ~9.6% after hours. The read across the group: the spend is genuinely huge and the companies are committed, but each dollar of AI capex is low-margin and slow to earn back, so the market is now marking down the whole complex on cash-flow and margin fear — not on demand.

The Fed

The FOMC held its policy rate at 3.50–3.75% on Wednesday, as expected — but the vote was split 9–3, with three regional Fed presidents dissenting in favour of a 0.25% hike. A hawkish hold, in other words: rates aren't coming down to rescue risk assets, and the July CPI print on 12 August now carries more weight for the next move.

China memory

The China competition story that hit Micron on Monday is still live: CXMT (ChangXin), China's state-backed DRAM maker, IPO'd this week and is scaling toward ~350k wafer-starts-a-month of DRAM capacity by year-end — approaching Micron's ~375k, though still a generation or two behind on the high-margin AI memory (HBM). It's a real threat in commodity DRAM and a genuine new bear input for the memory names, even as the HBM prize stays with the incumbents.

The capex gauntlet the earlier briefs kept pointing at has now been walked, and the verdict was 'the spend is too high for the payback we can see' — applied to the entire AI-infrastructure book, not one name. The washout has cooled every heat score, which is the setup patient buyers wait for; but with Amazon and Apple still to report tonight and no policy cushion from the Fed, this is a knife that is still falling, not a base that has formed.
