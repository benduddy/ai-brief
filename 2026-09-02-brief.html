Market backdrop

the setting
The setting

Tape

Risk-off, macro-driven. The S&P 500 slipped to ~7,631 (about 2% below its late-August record), the Nasdaq to ~26,100, and semiconductors led the drag. The cause is the Fed: after Warsh highlighted inflation, CME-implied odds of a September rate hike rose to ~66% from ~40% a week earlier. Layered on top: oil topped $95 on fresh US airstrikes on Iran, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose to ~4.80%. Credit, though, is still calm — high-yield spreads are tight at 2.63% and not deteriorating — so this reads as a rates/growth scare, not a credit event.

Macro regime

Neutral (unchanged) — so no regime call today. Credit is tight (not deteriorating), the dollar is firm, and while gold is diverging from real yields on a short horizon, gold itself sits below its 200-day, so the classifier does not read a debasement turn. Neutral is the expected state most days; it is not an all-clear, just the absence of a signal.

Liquidity gate (2s10s)

The yield-curve gate that Raoul Pal / GMI treat as the trigger for liquidity broadening into crypto and small-caps needs the curve to bull-steepen through ~45bps. Instead it has slipped from 0.48 to 0.40 and its 50-day trend flipped from steepening to flat — moving further shut, not toward open. A shut gate does not break the AI thesis; it just means the broadening trade hasn't started. Consistent with BTC and SOL rolling over this fortnight.

Falsifier watch (Sep monthly check)

Both AI-thesis falsifiers intact. F1 (GPU rental): committed/contract rates firm and capacity still tight — B200 median ~$6.52/hr, flat over 90 days and ~10% above a year ago; only on-demand posted prices are softening while contract prices hold, so no fire. F2 (DRAM spot): still rising — contract prices up strongly through 2026 with TrendForce/SemiAnalysis expecting the climb to continue into 2027. Nvidia doubling its supply commitments to $279B (mostly memory) is the same signal from the buyer's side. Neither has fired.

Sector leadership (numbers only)

Over the last 50 sessions, financials have outrun semiconductors by ~25 points and materials by ~19, while semis themselves are down ~18.5%; high-yield credit spreads remain tight (2.63%). Stated as fact — one leg of the late-cycle pattern is present (value leading) and the other (credit stress) is not, so no conclusion is drawn.

Portfolio vs index (fact, no conclusion)

The S&P sits ~2% below its record while the 58-name AI universe is, on average, ~39% below its own 60-day highs. Stated flat, no inference. (The 58-name figure includes lower-beta application-layer names and is not directly comparable to the old 50-name backtest drawdowns.)

Flexible-load watch (Sep monthly check)

One new item: CAISO published a straw proposal (12 Aug) adding two flexible large-load interconnection services and a 50 MW large-load definition, with comments due 2 Sep and a FERC filing due 16 Nov — a fourth non-ERCOT grid now moving (after SPP CHILLS, PJM RBP, MISO). This is a diffusion signal (everyone can connect flexibly), mildly bearish the equipment names over time, not an adopter win for any one operator. No new single-operator adopter this month.

What the smart money is saying (from the wiki)

Milk Road (11 Aug) called exactly this shape — consolidation, not a top: infrastructure names chopping 20–30% off highs while the data stays strong, with "fireworks in September–October." We are now in that window, and the tape is still consolidating. Jordi Visser (16 Aug) argues the buildout is memory-gated, not construction-gated — so delays push contracted revenue out rather than destroying it; Nvidia's $279B memory commitment is that claim from the other side of the table. InvestAnswers has flagged Micron's sub-$900 area as an accumulation zone — now reclaimed from below. The through-line: the operators who called the July bottom see this as digestion, not a break.
