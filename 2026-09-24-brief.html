What happened

US equities sold off on the completed session with the S&P ~−0.8% and the Nasdaq ~−1.1%, driven by rates rather than by anything AI-specific: the 10-year Treasury yield pushed above 5.11%, its highest since 2007, after a stronger-than-expected S&P Global PMI reading and firmer oil. Inside our 65-name universe the damage was not evenly spread. Memory & Storage (+4.1%), Semiconductors (+1.4%) and Power & Grid (+1.3%) rose; Crypto Tokens (−8.5%), Data Center & Networking (−3.6%), Solar & Energy (−3.5%), Application Layer (−3.2%) and Mega-cap (−2.0%) fell. 13 names changed status — the widest single-day shake-up in weeks.

The split, stated plainly

Money moved up the supply chain, toward whatever is physically scarce, and out of whatever has to buy it. The memory shortage is the stated reason: HBM revenue is forecast near $60B in 2026, up roughly 70% on 2025, with DRAM and NAND both tight. Against that, the two biggest AI spenders were sold on the mirror-image worry — that capex keeps rising while the revenue it is supposed to produce stays invisible. The tell that this is a rotation and not a break: the entry watchlist barely moved. Nine A-grades and eight B-grades remain, and almost all of them sit in semis, optics, memory and power.

Theme vs index

Worth noting the gap: the S&P is about 1.2% below its record high, while the 65-name AI universe sits about 21.3% below its own 60-day high, on average. Those are the numbers; draw no conclusion from them. (Caveat: the live average covers today's universe, which is not the one the historical drawdown figures were computed on — the two are not one series.)

Leadership check

This is the cheapest live test between two opposite forecasts that both expect the next few months to be up, so direction separates nothing and leadership does. A leg changed sign this week. Financials now trail semiconductors by about 2.8 points over the last 50 sessions, having led by roughly 17 points on 18 September; materials trail semis by 0.3 points; semis themselves are up 0.2% over that window. Credit is tight and improving — high-yield spreads at 2.68% on the real FRED series (not a proxy), 50-day change −0.03. Both legs therefore point the same way: this does not rhyme with David Hunter's late-cycle script, which needs financials and materials beating semis and credit deteriorating together. On his own framework he is early again, not right yet. Today's insurance weakness is the visible edge of that financials unwind — Allstate −7.1%, Travelers flipped Leading → Neutral, Chubb Lagging → Basing. One level worth naming because a source named it, not because the model derived it: IGV is at 108.1, against the ~110 Hunter flagged as a possible top. This is a scorecard on someone else's call, not a signal of our own.

Bearish

It fell 12.0% — the biggest single fall in the book, and worth 1.5 normal daily swings on its own. The identified cause is share supply, not operations: a 31 August S-3ASR registering 56.5M shares for resale by existing holders surfaced in filings awareness on the 23rd, and law firms announced investigations into potential wrongdoing by officers and directors aimed at pre-November-2025 buyers. Be precise about the mechanics — a resale registration raises no money for the company; it converts existing holders' stock into sellable stock, so it is an overhang on supply rather than fresh dilution. That distinction does not make it benign, but it is not the same thing. Underneath, the company lost $1.20 per share last quarter and is pre-profit. And the scale problem is structural: Tesla deployed 13.5 GWh in a single quarter and Fluence booked $1.44B of orders in one, against Eos's 3.4 GWh of backlog and 6.5 GWh discharged in its entire lifetime. It is a niche player in a market its own thesis describes.

Valuation

Forward P/E is negative (−20.6) and next-year growth screens at −97%, so there is no valuation read here at all — no PEG, no earnings multiple, nothing to anchor to. Say that plainly rather than reaching for a number: this is valued on backlog conversion and financing, and on nothing else.

Forward view

The Google/MN8 project is the first genuine counter-example, which is why it matters more than its size. Honest answer on whether the market has priced the future: at $3.58, 81% below its 52-week high, the market is pricing a meaningful probability that the conversion does not happen. That is a real option, not a bargain.
