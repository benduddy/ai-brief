Market Backdrop

the tape + the smart money

Where we are

This is a Monday pre-open snapshot, so the equity numbers are Friday 1 Aug's closing prices — nothing has traded since. Friday's session itself was earnings-driven, not jobs-driven (the July payrolls report is Friday 7 Aug, not last week). The AI complex is still in deep repair: zero overheated names for most of the past week, broken only now by Amazon; heat readings sit on the floor across memory, optical and the smaller semis, and almost the whole book is below its 50-day average. This is early base-building, not fresh froth — the open question stays 'real base or dead-cat', with most names yet to reclaim their trend lines.

Macro & semis

The Fed held at 3.50–3.75% on 30 Jul (9–3), and stocks fell on the lack of a clear cut signal. Markets now price roughly a 54% chance of a 25bp cut at the 16 Sep meeting (CME FedWatch) — notably, that's the market leaning toward easing even as a followed source reads new-chair Warsh as tilting hawkish (see below). Under the surface the semiconductor story is intact: 15 consecutive monthly records in global chip sales, and the 'capex is demand, not waste' framing gained ground after the Microsoft, Meta and Amazon cloud prints. The fresh worry is competitive — China's Moonshot unveiled Kimi K3, a large open-weight model, nudging the US-AI-dominance debate. Net: a fundamentals-driven, stock-picker's phase rather than a one-way trade.

What the smart money is saying (from the wiki)

Jordi Visser (26 Jul): 'short-term AI fear, long-term compute scarcity' — the washout is a technical flush, not a demand failure; crypto stays a bear market until Bitcoin reclaims its 200-day, and his retail-energy gauge (Dogecoin below its 20-day for a record ~67 days) says retail is absent. Where they diverge: both are structurally bullish and near-term cautious, but IA is more willing to nibble equities here, while Jordi's focus is the multi-year compute-scarcity payoff over the next tactical bounce.

If AWS holds a 30%+ growth path and that $496B backlog converts, the 2–3-year earnings base steps up materially. But the market has clearly started pricing the re-acceleration — this is no longer an under-the-radar story, so today's price already leans on that future arriving.
