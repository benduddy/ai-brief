What happened Friday

The second leg of the relief was selective. After Thursday's broad snapback (where the harder a name had fallen, the harder it bounced), Friday's gains concentrated in the mega-cap winners: Amazon ~+15% on its cloud results, Alphabet ~+7%, Nvidia ~+3.5%, Microsoft and Meta ~+3% each. Apple was the standout loser, down ~7% on soft guidance. The index level was calm (S&P +0.7%) but underneath it the tape was sorting — money moving toward the names posting genuine numbers and away from the ones that just guided softly. In model terms, that's why the book didn't lift uniformly: a few names ran hot while the deep-discount cohort actually gave a bit back.

Why Amazon overheated — the AWS print

This is what tipped the first heat score over the line in a week. Amazon's Q2 (reported Thursday after the close, with the full move landing Friday) showed AWS revenue past $42B on 37% growth — its fastest in 18 quarters — total revenue ~$200.6B (+20%), and earnings per share of $5.75 against a ~$1.81 estimate. The one caveat sits in the cash flow: trailing free cash flow swung to a ~$7.6B outflow as the company leans hard into AI-and-cloud capex. So the demand signal for the whole data-center book (cloud growth re-accelerating) is strongly positive; the cost of it is aggressive spending that the market, for now, is happy to look past.

The weekend + the week ahead

Over the weekend, with equities closed, Bitcoin slipped below the level that had it screening Leading and dropped back to Basing — the only fresh signal in the book. The calendar from here is dense: Arista reports Tuesday (4 Aug) after the close; July CPI lands 12 Aug and carries extra weight because the Fed held rates on a hawkish 9–3 vote last week; Marvell reports 20 Aug; Jackson Hole runs 22–24 Aug; and Nvidia's own quarter (26 Aug) is the bellwether print for the entire AI-capex debate.
