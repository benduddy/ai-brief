TAPE · AI-trade & macro backdrop
Week ending 25 Jul

Stocks

A quiet, choppy week: the S&P 500 slipped about 0.6% and the Nasdaq 100 about 1.5%. The pattern this summer has been two weeks up, two down, two up — a market digesting a big move higher, not one falling apart. Tech momentum actually bounced ~8% this week after what Jordi called "a gruesome four weeks," helped in large part by Google's post-earnings drop dragging the hyperscaler group.

Capex fear

The thing spooking people is the bill for the AI build-out. Combined hyperscaler capital-spending plans for 2026 have swelled toward ~$770B, debt issuance to fund it is running roughly four times last year's pace, and the credit spreads on that debt are widening (Oracle's default-insurance cost has more than tripled since last autumn). This is the "AI fear" backdrop — real, but Jordi frames it as liquidity being soaked up by issuance during a low-volume summer, not the boom ending.

Rates & oil

The Fed decides Wednesday 29 Jul. A hold at 3.50–3.75% is widely expected, but with no fresh guidance about a third of the market now prices a possible hike. Oil has pushed back toward the high-$70s on renewed US–Iran tension; Jordi's point is that the inflation market isn't buying the scare — two-year inflation expectations haven't budged. The next CPI reading lands 12 Aug.

WIKI · What the smart money is saying
From the wiki + today's fresh Jordi video

Jordi Visser (26 Jul, new)

His whole thesis today: separate short-term noise from long-term flow. He thinks the AI stocks chop for the rest of the year, but the secular bull market is intact (the market is still above its rising 200-day average). Crucially, he's a buyer into this weakness — "when people get bearish on AI again, that's when I start getting more interested" — and he started buying last week. The long-term anchor is compute scarcity: cheaper models just create more demand (Jevons paradox), so "we will forever be short compute." That's his case for the receivers of the spend — memory and optical/chip names — over the hyperscaler spenders.

Where he's leaning

Marvell remains his single biggest position (betting on an optical/photonics "step-up" in coming earnings), and he bought another optical name (AAOI) this week. On Google he's constructive despite the cash-flow shock — see its line below. On crypto he's patient: Bitcoin is "stable around $64k" but still in a bear phase until it reclaims its 200-day, and he's watching Dogecoin's 20-day average (below it a record ~67 days) as the sign retail energy has returned. This is the same picture as the wiki's ai-trade-rotation and memory-chips pages — capex punishes spenders near-term while receivers wait to be paid.
