The tape, the regime, and the smart money

The day

A broad AI-trade de-risking. Semis (SMH) fell ~5–6% and the Nasdaq was off modestly; within the 58-name universe there were 56 row-changes and 16 status flips, almost all out of Leading. The proximate trigger reads as AI-sentiment — widely-circulated 'slow down / safety' commentary that traces to a single aggregated source, so treat the attribution as thin — compounded by a jump in crude oil. Nothing in it looks like a demand break; the strongest names (NVIDIA, Micron) only pulled back to their 50-day lines while the more crowded, higher-beta trades (Corning, Coherent, Astera, Lumentum) took the real damage.

Macro regime

The model's regime call changed today, from debasement back to neutral — and the change is the point. Per the capital-rotation framework, debasement (gold rising into rising nominal yields with a soft dollar) is the thesis-friendly backdrop; what removed it is simply that the dollar is no longer soft — it now sits ~0.5% above its 200-day — even though gold keeps diverging from real yields (10-yr real ~2.6%). Credit is calm (high-yield spreads ~2.65%, near multi-year lows, not deteriorating) and volatility is contained (VIX ~17). This reads on FRED's real credit and rate series, so it can be stated plainly rather than hedged. The discipline stays the same in both directions: neutral is the expected output most days, including the day before a turn, and it is not an all-clear; a regime call is a 'pay attention' flag, never an exit signal.
