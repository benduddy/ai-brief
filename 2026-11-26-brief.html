Chips drop 8% by mid-morning, then buy back almost all of it

26 November 2026 · 68 names · benchmark QQQ · data Wed 25 Nov close · published 06:06 UTC

Wednesday was a round trip. By 11:00 ET the Nasdaq-100 fund (QQQ) was down 4.4% and the chip index (SOX) was down 8%. By the close the SOX was down only 0.7%, the Nasdaq 0.6% and the S&P 500 0.25%. In our universe, 44 of 60 names closed in the top 15% of their day's range. The fall had named causes: a bank cut its 2028 memory-price forecasts, Ambarella blamed memory costs for a weak guide, the US proposed new tariffs, and core inflation was hot. I found no dated cause for the recovery. US markets are closed today for Thanksgiving.

  • Macro regime: deflationary-stress, day 8 — unchanged. High-yield spreads 3.72% (FRED), up from 3.63% and +0.51 points in 50 days. VIX 28.3. The dollar is 3.7% above its 200-day; the real 10-year rate is 2.79%. After hot core PCE, futures and Kalshi still price a December Fed hike at ~92–96%. A pay-attention flag, not an exit signal.
  • Data (25 Nov): core PCE +0.3% m/m, 3.3% y/y vs 3.2% expected — the highest since Nov 2023. Q3 GDP revised up to 4.3% (3.5% expected). Jobless claims 189k (200k expected). Strong growth plus sticky inflation is the mix that keeps a hike on the table.
  • Memory scare: an unnamed “major US bank” now sees conventional DRAM prices falling 19–20% in 2028 (it had −5%), NAND −21% and HBM down more than 20%, while it still sees DRAM +32% in 2027 (Investing.com, 25 Nov). Micron was down ~13% at the low and closed −4.5% at $601.25. This is a forecast, not a spot price: falsifier F2 (DRAM spot) stays intact.
  • Chip tools also carried a China-policy worry: reports tied the Lam (−5.7%) and Applied (−3.9%) falls to the MATCH Act tool-curb bill, but I found no new step on the bill since April. UBS cut its Applied target to $575 from $640 (Buy kept, 25 Nov).
  • Nvidia −3.9% to $149.30, down two days in a row after Monday's +31.5%. New: S&P Global put Nvidia's AA rating on CreditWatch negative (25 Nov), citing board oversight and key-person risk after Huang's return. Still no 10-Q and no new SEC item. Hearings: 1 Dec (House, closed) and 3 Dec (Senate Banking).
  • Amazon flips Leading → Neutral and closes just under its 200-day (~$245.78) at $244.43. It drops from entry grade C to D. Bernstein says web traffic on usage-based cloud platforms, AWS included, runs ~16% below Q3 levels in mid–late November.
  • The only leaders left are insurers. ALL, CB and TRV hold grade A, all within 1% of their 52-week highs. No company news; this fits the defensive leadership of the past seven weeks.
