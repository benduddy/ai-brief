What happened

Thursday was a violent snapback. After four-plus sessions of selling that had washed every heat score down to the floor, the AI-trade complex reversed hard — and the beaten names led it: crypto miners up 20–30%, memory and optical up 15–18%, the big semis up 9–13%. In model terms almost the whole 48-name book gained, momentum readings (the strength of the recent move) jumped across the board, and yet heat is still near zero everywhere — the bounce started from such a washed-out base that one huge day barely lifts the 'stretched' gauge. Nothing is overheated.

The pivot — Microsoft

This is what turned the tape. Microsoft reported Wednesday after the close with its Azure cloud growing ~43% year-on-year and crossing $100B in annual revenue, and it presented a large FY2027 capital-spending step-up (up ~35%) as a confident bet on compounding AI demand rather than a panicked splurge. That directly answered the week's fear: instead of 'the hyperscalers are over-spending and can't earn it back', the read flipped to 'the demand is real enough to justify the spend'. The semiconductor index rose ~8% Thursday and Asian memory makers (Samsung, SK Hynix) jumped 20–25% overnight.

Amazon + Apple

Thursday night's second batch of mega-cap prints was mixed, which is why this is a bounce and not a clean all-clear. Amazon's AWS cloud came in strong — a direct, positive read on data-center spending pace, and a tailwind for NVDA, DELL, VRT and the networking names. Apple guided softer. So the demand signal that matters most for the AI-infrastructure book (cloud growth) is holding up, but the earnings season underneath the rally is uneven, not uniformly strong.

The Fed

No policy cushion here. The FOMC held its rate at 3.50–3.75% on Wednesday, but the vote split 9–3 with three regional Fed presidents dissenting in favour of a 0.25% hike — a hawkish hold. Rates aren't coming down to underwrite risk assets, so the next inflation read (July CPI, 12 August) carries extra weight, and this relief trade stays vulnerable to a hot print or a hawkish Jackson Hole (22–24 Aug).

Read

The capex fear that drove the washout got a real counter-argument this week — Microsoft's numbers said the spending is demand, not waste — and the most oversold names mechanically snapped back the hardest. That's a genuine improvement in the story, not just a technical bounce. But the honest caveat is that almost everything is still below its 50-day line and heat is on the floor: this is the first green day of a possible repair, with an uneven earnings season and a hawkish Fed underneath it. Reclaiming those short-term lines is what turns a bounce into a base.

Forward view

The forward engine is the AI-server backlog — orders pulled forward by enterprise and cloud buildouts. Whether that converts is the whole debate: if the backlog is mostly low-margin GPU boxes, the profit line lags the revenue line. Thursday's strong AWS print is a good sign the underlying spend is holding; Dell's own Q2 in early September is the read that matters for whether the backlog is still growing. Today's price has repaired most of the crack — the market is no longer pricing a demand collapse, but hasn't resolved the margin question either.
